\section{Cultivation}
Cultivating involves absorbing ambient Qi from the surrounding space normally through meditative breathing. This Qi is then placed within the dantian where it is then circulated through one's meridians. The dantian is like a muscle and through training it can be made to store and release more Qi. The meridians act like canals of water connecting the entire body, clean and open meridians allow for more controlled flow in larger quantities. 

The hardships of a person add contaminants to their meridians which hampers their ability to circulate Qi. By circulating Qi through these contaminants they slowly carry the impurities out through the body. Too much Qi in a single area can have catastrophic consequences. 

There are 12 main meridians (Lung, Large intestine, Stomach, Heart, Small intestine, Bladder, Kidney, Pericardium, Triple Energizer, Gallbladder, Liver) \& 8 energy meridians (Conception, Governing, Penetrating, Girdle, Yin Linking, Yang Linking, Yin Heel, Yang Heel)

Unblocking these meridians increases your cultivation stage. Going from a Qi Gathering 0 cultivator with no opened meridians to Energy Storing 8 with a total of 20 meridians opened.

Cultivating is by its very nature a energy demanding activity, especially in terms of calories. A Cultivator who dedicates a whole week to cultivation eats up to a month worth of food.

In normal, unremarkable places in the world a regular cultivator gains 1d6 worth of Qi per Cultivation Stage + 1 for every week spent cultivating. This assumes this is the main activity of that week. Multiple cultivators absorbing Qi or a lifeless area will hamper this while a bountiful waterfall will aid it instead.

If the area is suffused with Qi of an advantageous element or the density is high enough the die size can grow to 1d8, but the opposite is also possible.

Your Qi can be used in all sorts of ways but using it diverts it from progressing in one's cultivation. Whenever you gain Qi while at your limit you can expand it by using half of the Qi absorbed.

There are pills, plants \& potions that will fill your body with Qi with the potential downside of Qi deviation depending on the material's purity and alignment to your own Qi.

If your stored Qi are above your limit for more than a moment of time it will overflow your meridians which can be debilitating if not fatal.

\subsection{Qi elements}
Certain types of Qi come with alignments and so do cultivation methods. Aligning yourself with an element gives you the potential of overpowering stronger foes but also runs the risk of succumbing to weaker enemies. 

\subsubsection{Wuxing}

There are five elements in total, Fire, Water, Wood, Metal \& Earth. There are two cycles which dictate their interactions. 

\textbf{The generative cycle}: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Wood feeds Fire as fuel
    \item Fire produces Earth (ash, lava)
    \item Earth bears Metal (geological processes produce minerals)
    \item Metal collects, filters and purifies Water (water vapour condenses on metal, for example)
    \item Water nourishes Wood (water leads to growth of flowers, plants and other changes in nature)
\end{itemize}

\textit{Example 1: A cultivator whose Qi is aligned with Wood Qi will feel their punches land softer against a Fire aligned cultivator than an unaligned cultivator}

\textit{Example 2: A cultivator whose Qi is aligned with Metal will be able to cultivate easier within a mountain than most places}

\textbf{The destructive cycle}: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Wood grasps (or stabilizes) Earth (roots of trees can prevent soil erosion)
    \item Earth contains (or directs) Water (dams or river banks)
    \item Water dampens (or regulates) Fire
    \item Fire melts (or refines or shapes) Metal
    \item Metal chops (or carves) Wood
\end{itemize}

\textit{Example 1: A cultivator whose Qi is aligned with Wood Qi will hit harder against an earth-aspected cultivator than an unaligned cultivator}

\textit{Example 2: A cultivator whose Qi is aligned with Fire Qi can not easily digest a water-aspected spirit herb which usually boosts healing}

\subsubsection{Yin \& Yang}

Qi in every element comes in a range of Yin \& Yang, Moon \& Sun, Death \& Life. People more than most sources of Qi are aspected in this way, men are infused with Yang, 
women are infused with Yin. This is not a hard rule since every person have both Yin \& Yang organs \& meridians. 

An unbalance between your Yin \& Yang can cause illnesses among the organs. A surplus of Yin Qi will feed your Yang organs and neglect your Yin organs and vice versa. 

Some cultivation methods focus more on the Yin \& Yang balance either within one self to create a wave motion to fuel each side evenly or 
duo cultivating where you share Qi with a partner usually through sexual acts. Duo cultivating is most effective for both parties if the exchange is mutual, it is also 
possible for one party to drain the other (or multiple others) for their Qi.

\subsubsection{Qi infusion}

A cultivator can move stored Qi from their dantian into an organ of choice boosting its function for a short burst.
This consumes Qi which would otherwise be able to be used towards cultivation

\subsubsection{Qi sense}
A cultivator can attune their senses to the very nature of Qi to sense the presence of or cultivation level of another

\subsubsection{Qi obfuscation}
A cultivator can try to hide their presence and downplay their cultivation and killing intent to avoid giving away information or pass beneath another's view

\subsection{Intermediate Qi abilities}

\subsubsection{Qi Nourishments}
A Cultivator can substitute consuming food with spending absorbed Qi. It takes about 30 Qi every week to keep a Cultivator from starving. 
Thrice that if they are cultivating.

\subsubsection{Killing intent Manifestation}
A Cultivator can infuse their killing intent with Qi to intimidate, incapacitate or even kill lower cultivators
This consumes Qi which would otherwise be able to be used towards cultivation

\subsubsection{Qi Blade}
A Cultivator can enhance their blade's edge to deal devastating amounts of damage.
This consumes Qi which would otherwise be able to be used towards cultivation

\subsection{Advanced Qi Abilities}
\subsubsection{Qi Sword}
A Cultivator can forgo wielding a weapon at all and conjure a weapon of pure Qi instead of any physical material.
This consumes Qi which would otherwise be able to be used towards cultivation
